A Simple Guide to SIP, EMI and Income Tax

For students, salaried professionals and first-time investors.

1. How a Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) Works

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) lets you invest a fixed amount at regular intervals, usually every month, into a mutual fund. Because you invest every month, you buy more units when prices are low and fewer units when prices are high. This is called rupee cost averaging, and it reduces the need to time the market.

Over many years your returns also earn returns. This is compounding, and it is why starting early matters more than investing a large amount later.

Formula used:
M = P × [ (1 + i)^n − 1 ] × (1 + i) / i
M is the maturity amount, P is the monthly investment, i is the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of months.

The return rate you enter is only an assumption. Real mutual fund returns go up and down and are not guaranteed.

2. Understanding Loan EMI

An Equated Monthly Instalment (EMI) is the fixed amount you pay your lender every month. Each EMI has two parts: principal and interest. In the early years most of the EMI goes towards interest, and over time more of it goes towards principal.

Before taking a loan, check that your total EMIs stay within a comfortable share of your monthly take-home income. Many lenders prefer that total EMIs do not go above roughly 40% to 50% of income, and a lower share is safer.

Formula used:
EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n / [ (1 + r)^n − 1 ]
P is the loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate and n is the number of monthly instalments.

3. Income Tax Under the New Regime (FY 2026-27)

Under the new tax regime, income up to ₹4 lakh has no tax. After that the rates are 5% (₹4–8 lakh), 10% (₹8–12 lakh), 15% (₹12–16 lakh), 20% (₹16–20 lakh), 25% (₹20–24 lakh) and 30% above ₹24 lakh. Salaried taxpayers can reduce ₹75,000 as standard deduction before applying these slabs.

The Section 87A rebate removes the tax completely if your taxable income is up to ₹12 lakh, which means salaried income up to about ₹12.75 lakh. If your income is slightly above ₹12 lakh, marginal relief makes sure the tax is not more than the amount by which you cross ₹12 lakh. A 4% health and education cess is added on the tax.

Tax rules can change in every Budget, and your own situation (old regime, other income, capital gains) may be different. Use this tool as a quick estimate, and confirm with the Income Tax Department website or a tax professional before filing.

4. Building a Strong Financial Base

A sound plan usually has three parts: an emergency fund that covers about six months of expenses, term life and health insurance, and long-term investments linked to your goals. Use these calculators to see how your monthly amounts add up before you commit.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my data stored anywhere?

No. All calculations happen in your browser. The numbers you type are not sent to our servers.

Is SIP return guaranteed?

No. The calculator uses the rate you enter as an assumption. Actual market-linked returns can be higher or lower.

Does the EMI include processing fees or insurance?

No. It only shows the EMI from the loan amount, interest rate and tenure. Fees and insurance are extra.

Why is my tax zero?

Under the new regime, taxable income up to ₹12 lakh gets a full rebate under Section 87A. For salaried people this is about ₹12.75 lakh of income after the ₹75,000 standard deduction.

Does the tax estimator cover the old regime?

Not at the moment. It covers the new regime only, without surcharge, capital gains or special-rate income.
